A worked hour

What the file actually looks like, and where the common answer goes wrong.

09:04

The call

Delaware incorporated, offices in Austin. A telehealth platform, so a HIPAA covered entity. Unauthorised access discovered this morning. Their general counsel asks the reasonable first question: does New York reach us at all, given we have nothing there?

09:11

Nexus

New York's breach statute does not have a nexus test in the usual sense.

The intuition: no office, no employees, no revenue in the state, so no obligation. Look for the threshold.

There is no threshold. GBL § 899-aa applies to "any person or business which owns or licenses computerized data which includes private information". No office, no headcount, no revenue floor appears in the text. One New York patient is enough.

09:19

Preemption

They are a covered entity, so the next question is whether HIPAA displaces the state duty.

It does not. 45 C.F.R. § 160.203 makes HIPAA a floor, so more stringent state law survives alongside it. The two regimes run in parallel, and where the deadlines differ the shorter one governs in practice.

09:26

The clock nobody starts

They notified the Secretary of HHS at 09:15, which was correct and prompt.

The natural plan: work the HIPAA sixty day notice, then the New York thirty day notice, in that order.

That filing started a shorter clock. GBL § 899-aa(9) requires notice to the New York Attorney General within five business days of notifying the Secretary. Not five days from discovery. By day six of a sixty day plan, the first deadline is already missed.

09:34

The pathway that does not rescue it

Counsel finds § 899-aa(2)(b), which deems notice given under another regime sufficient.

It is an activity-level carve-out, not an entity-level exemption. It removes the duty to notify people who were already notified federally. It expressly preserves notice to the Attorney General, the Department of State and the State Police. The entity stays in scope for everything else in the section.

09:41

Exposure

The question the client will ask next.

Attorney General enforcement. No private right of action under § 899-aa. For a knowing or reckless violation, a civil penalty of the greater of $5,000 or up to $20 per instance of failed notification, capped at $250,000, plus damages for actual costs or losses. GBL § 349 remains the private vehicle.

Four ways a confident answer goes wrong

These are the failure modes this project was built against. They are listed in the standing brief, and each one has test scenarios written specifically to catch it.

A citation that does not say that

plausible section, wrong content

The section exists, the number is right, and the text says something else. Here every obligation carries the verbatim span and the hash of the bytes it was checked against, so the quotation is auditable rather than trusted.

A law that is not law yet

enacted, not in force, reported as binding

Commentary is dense with laws that were signed but do not bind yet, and with bills that never passed at all. Status is a recorded field here, checked before dates are, so pending law can never enter an obligation result at any date.

An exemption applied to the wrong thing

entity-level and data-level collapsed

A hospital is a covered entity for a patient chart and not for the same nurse's pre-employment physical. Same entity, same person, different capacity. Exemptions are typed objects here, and a data-level carve-out never removes the entity from scope.

Silence read as permission

"no statute applies"

When no sectoral statute reaches a practice, the answer is not nothing. FTC Act § 5, the state deceptive practices statute, and the company's own published privacy notice still constrain it. Those are returned on every query, without exception.

Do not trust it. Check it.

Every record answers with the statutory text, the URL it was fetched from, the date it was fetched, the hash of those exact bytes, and the dates it was in force. If the corpus does not have a citation, it refuses instead of producing one.

Two hashes are kept, and they are different things. One covers the source bytes, which never change. The other covers the segmentation, the reading of those bytes into numbered provisions, which changes whenever the text parser changes. That second hash exists because a parser fix silently re-cuts every provision underneath every record drawn from that source. It caught a real defect: a record citing Penal Law § 250.00(1) while quoting the words of § 250.00(2).

Where most of the work went

What it will not tell you

A confident answer over a gap is worse than a smaller answer that names the gap. These refusals are enforced in code, not by convention.

It will not answer without a date

There is no current law here, only law as of a date. A malformed date is refused too, because date comparisons are string comparisons: a typo would otherwise sort above every real date and quietly report law that is not yet in force. as_of: "not-a-date" → refused

It will not treat enacted law as binding early

The SAFE for Kids Act is enacted, final, and binds nobody until 25 January 2027. It routes to a watch feed that cannot become an obligation at any date you ask about, because status is checked before dates are. status: enacted_pending → watch feed only

It will not hide what it cannot reach

Coverage is declared before it is measured, so an instrument knows which duty categories are missing and says so inside the answer. The SAFE for Kids result is honest and incomplete: what "commercially reasonable and technically feasible" requires is decided in a regulation this corpus does not hold.

It will not guess a preemption posture

Floor, ceiling, field, express partial, or none. An unrecognised value used to fall through to "none", which is the most permissive answer available and the one direction a preemption engine must never guess in. It now returns unresolved and says it is not a finding of no preemption.

About five minutes

Ask it in plain English

It installs into Claude Desktop, so you type the question the way you would ask a colleague. There is a terminal command as well, if that is your preference.

Install Node

The program that runs it. Download the version marked LTS from nodejs.org and click through the installer. Version 20 or newer. Nothing to configure.

Get the project, open a terminal in that folder

On a Mac, open Terminal, type cd and a space, then drag the folder from Finder into the window and press Return. On Windows, open the folder, click the address bar, type cmd and press Enter.

git clone https://github.com/rakib-nyc/privacy-kb.git cd privacy-kb

Two commands, then restart Claude Desktop

The second finds the Claude Desktop settings file on your machine and adds the connection for you. Then quit Claude Desktop completely, not just the window, and reopen it.

npm install npm run setup -- --write # drop --write to print it instead

Ask something you can check

The useful test is whether it cites a section or answers from general knowledge.

We are a telehealth company in Texas with one patient in New York. We had a breach on 8 September and notified HHS the same day. What are our deadlines?

You should get five business days to the New York Attorney General under § 899-aa(9), and an explanation that notifying HHS is what started it.

If anything is off, ask it to check itself

Five named checks. It tells you which one failed rather than reporting an error.

npm run doctor

Where it is weak

Published because a tool that hides these is less trustworthy than one that does not. The full list, with 24 findings from five adversarial passes, is in the repository.

Business days mean weekdays

Public holidays are not excluded, so a clock crossing one lands a day early. Every affected result says so in the output. Confirm any deadline that crosses a holiday.

Pending law does not promote itself

A record stays marked pending after its effective date until a person updates it. Nothing silently becomes law, which is the point. It also means the corpus goes stale on 25 January 2027 unless it is maintained.

New York only, on the state side

No other state. No GDPR beyond transfer-mechanism touchpoints. Almost no case law: three constitutional doctrines are held, unverified, and never surfaced.

Coverage is 31 of 69 subject areas

38 areas are neither covered nor ruled out of scope. That number is tracked and may only go down. Nine instruments hold a single record each: complete against what they declare, but thin.
